Why does "Stacking" matter?

FODMAP stands for Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols. Individually, a portion may represent a low concentration of e.g. fructans. However, when eating several foods with low-to-moderate fructans at the same meal, they stack inside your small intestine, absorbing excess water and generating rapid fermentation by colonic bacteria.

Pantry Staples Cheat Sheet

Essential gluten-free and low-FODMAP safe substitutions for clinical dietary management.

🍞 Baking & Grain Staples
  • Gluten-Free Bread Trap:

    Avoid breads thickened with Inulin, Chicory Root, or containing apple fiber, high fructose corn syrup, or honey.

  • Flour Alternatives:

    Stock White/Brown Rice Flour, Tapioca Starch, Buckwheat Flour, and Potato Starch for self-baking.

  • Grains on Standby:

    Quinoa, White Rice, and Polenta are excellent, easy-to-digest low-FODMAP, gluten-free carbs with zero stacking risks.

🧄 Alliums & Seasonings Hack
  • Garlic-Infused Oil Miracle:

    Garlic contains high amounts of Fructans which are highly water-soluble but NOT oil-soluble. Olive oil infused with garlic is 100% low-FODMAP and safe!

  • Spring Onion Green Tops:

    The green stalks of scallions/spring onions are completely low-FODMAP, while the white bulb is packed with fructans. Slice off and throw away the white bulb.

  • Safe Spice Checklist:

    Ginger, Cumin, Coriander, Turmeric, Paprika, Oregano, Thyme, Rosemary, and Basil are 100% safe. Always check pre-mixed taco/curry spices for hidden onion/garlic powders!

🍯 Sweeteners & Fats Guide
  • Glucose vs Fructose Balance:

    Standard table sugar (sucrose) contains balanced parts of glucose and fructose. Glucose assists fructose absorption in the gut. Therefore, white table sugar is safe in moderation, unlike honey or agave!

  • Polyol Sweetener Warnings:

    Do not use keto-friendly or sugar-free foods containing Sorbitol, Xylitol, Mannitol, Maltitol, or Isomalt. These hold extreme osmotic loads and trigger fermentation.

  • Safe Liquid Sweeteners:

    100% Pure Maple Syrup is safe because of low fructose concentration. Rice malt syrup is also an excellent low-FODMAP substitute.

The Science of FODMAP Stacking

Understanding compound accumulation in irritable bowel syndromes.

What are FODMAPs?

FODMAP stands for Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides, and Polyols. These are short-chain carbohydrates (sugars) that the human digestive system sometimes struggles to fully absorb in the small intestine. Because they are poorly absorbed, they travel down into the large intestine where they serve as food for intestinal flora, undergoing rapid fermentation. This process releases gas and draws water into the colon, causing painful swelling, cramping, and bloating.

The 5 Core Biochemical Compounds

1. Fructans (Oligosaccharide) Found in wheat flour, garlic, onions, chicory root/inulin. Very common gut irritant.
2. GOS (Galacto-oligosaccharides) Found in pulses, almonds, cashews, and un-rinsed canned black beans/lentils.
3. Excess Fructose (Monosaccharide) Fructose is only absorbed well in equal ratio to glucose. Apples, pears, honey have excess fructose.
4. Lactose (Disaccharide) The sugar present in cow's milk, cream cheese, soft cheeses. Easily managed with lactose-free products.
5. Polyols (Sugar Alcohols) Sorbitol & Mannitol found in avocados, celery, stone fruits, or keto sweetener replacements like xylitol.

What is Stacking & How Do I Prevent It?

Many ingredients contain low concentrations of these compounds in small portions. However, if you combine broccoli heads, green beans, and almonds in one meal—each containing a safe portion of Sorbitol/Polyols or GOS—the sum of these compounds triggers the exact same symptoms as eating a high-FODMAP food.

To prevent stacking:

  • Space meals with moderate-to-high FODMAP categories at least 3-4 hours apart.
  • When mixing vegetables of the same FODMAP category, reduce their portions to 0.5x.
  • Include plenty of completely "FODMAP-Free" filler foods like white rice, chicken breast, carrots, eggs, and potatoes.
